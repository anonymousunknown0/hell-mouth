// SCENE: DEGENERATE RADIO – PART 1

SPONSORED BY: “MAKE MONEY ONLINE, IT’S EASY”

INT. DEGENERATE RADIO STUDIO – NIGHT

Hunter Hardon lounges in a torn office chair, lazily squeezing a light-up stress ball—rainbow LEDs pulse between his fingers.  
His eyes roll skyward as a **synth-heavy, aggressively cheerful JINGLE** blasts, almost drowning out the hiss of studio static.

INSERT:  
The battered studio monitor plays a *fake, vintage infomercial*—washed-out VHS colors, fake testimonials, ugly fonts.  
BANNERS scroll and blink:
        “MAKE MONEY ONLINE! IT’S EASY!”
        “FIRE YOUR BOSS! WORK IN YOUR UNDERWEAR!”
        “NO EXPERIENCE NECESSARY!”
        “CLICK HERE NOW!”

                        HUNTER (into mic, way too chipper)
            Tonight’s broadcast is sponsored by everyone’s favorite scam:
            Make money online—it’s easy!
            Yes, you too can go from burnout to billionaire
            with nothing but a phone, a ring light,
            and a complete disregard for your own dignity!

Hunter slaps a button—**CANNED APPLAUSE** erupts, then instantly warps into broken static, distorting into *sinister laughter*.

                        HUNTER (deadpan, mocking)
            All you need to do is…
            click the link in my bio,
            sign up for my 12-part “secrets of success” webinar,
            and sell your soul—or at least your personal data—
            for a shot at financial freedom!

He drops the bit mid-sentence, the smile sliding off.  
The LED ball dims in his hand.

                        HUNTER (raw, honest, breaking the wall)
            You want to know the real secret?
            The people getting rich are the ones selling you the dream.
            The rest of us are just scrolling, searching,
            “manifesting” some future that’s never coming.

                        HUNTER (soft, conspiratorial)
            But hey…
            if you figure it out,
            let me know.

ON MONITOR:  
The “MAKE MONEY ONLINE!” banner glitches, flickers, mutates into a **QR code that never fully loads**—just endless spinning pixels, a digital dead end.

FADE TO:

HELL-MOUTH  
FORCED INTO ISOLATION / ASMR-STYLE SPOKEN WORD / AWARENESS VS. UNAWARENESS

INT. DEGENERATE RADIO STUDIO – LATE NIGHT

Hunter sits in the half-dark, hunched over the mic, arms wrapped around himself.  
The studio feels smaller—walls closing in, gear humming with invisible heat.

A battered contact list glows on a flickering monitor. All names are crossed out.

                        HUNTER (into mic, slow, confessional)
            They say loneliness is a choice.  
            That you can just—reach out, make a call,
            crash someone’s dinner party and start your life over.
            That’s a lie.

                        HUNTER (choked, honest)
            I lost every friend I ever had.
            Some to distance.  
            Most to betrayal.
            My mouth said too much, or not enough.
            My brain—broadcasting at the wrong frequency.

INSERT – QUICK FLASHES:

—Hunter’s phone vibrating, unanswered.  
—A door slamming.  
—A group chat going silent.  
—Hunter alone at a table, empty glasses, empty chairs.

                        HUNTER (quiet, hopeless)
            I built my own cell, one blocked number at a time.
            Sometimes I wonder if I was born for exile.
            Or if I just practiced it so long, it felt like fate.

                        HUNTER (soft, desperate)
            If you’re listening—if you remember me—  
            I’m sorry.
            I really am.

LONG SILENCE.  
A faint hum. Static buzz.

                        NARRATOR (V.O., soothing, surreal)
            Tonight’s feature:
            ASMR-Style Spoken Word.
            For the insomniacs, the voyeurs,
            the ones whose bodies remember touch only as a rumor.

Hunter leans close to the mic, whisper-quiet:

                        HUNTER (ASMR-style, hypnotic)
            Can you hear me?  
            I’m tracing the outline of your name with my tongue.
            I’m breathing in the memory of a room you left behind.
            The click of a lighter, the exhale, the pulse behind your ear.
            I used to believe I could be forgiven.
            I used to believe I could forgive myself.

                        HUNTER (ASMR-style, poetic)
            Here’s the sound of a heart breaking,  
            listen:  
            (he taps his chest, slow and hollow)
            Here’s the sound of hope—  
            (he cracks his knuckles, exhales)
            Here’s the sound of wanting to be seen.
            (he breathes into the mic, trembling)
            Here’s the sound of me,  
            not gone yet.

                        NARRATOR (V.O., lullaby)
            Shhhhh.  
            You’re not alone.  
            You’re just awake at the wrong hour.

Hunter exhales, sits up straighter. His tone shifts.

                        HUNTER (clear, bracing)
            Let’s talk about being aware.
            Everyone says: be conscious, be present, own your shit.
            But what about the nights you didn’t even know what you were doing?

                        HUNTER (rising, defensive)
            I was unaware, not evil.
            I was lost, not malicious.
            Sometimes, you don’t get to choose what you remember.
            Sometimes, you don’t even get to choose what you are.

                        HUNTER (final, proud)
            I can’t be guilty for the crimes of someone who wasn’t there to enjoy it.
            I won’t carry stones that don’t belong to me.
            So if you need someone to blame,
            find someone who knew what they were doing.
            I’m done begging for forgiveness for things I never saw coming.

He turns off the mic.  
A long, humming silence.  
Just breath and static.

FADE TO BLACK.

HELL-MOUTH  
CHAPTER: HUNTER EXPOSES THE PHANTOM COMMUNICATION SYSTEM  
(HALLUCINATION-HEAVY DRAFT)

INT. RADIO STUDIO – NIGHT

The wall clock is stuck at 3:33 AM, its hands twitching back and forth, refusing to move forward.

The red “ON AIR” light glitches—flickering, then splitting into double vision:  
"ON AIR" — "OFF AIR"

Hunter Hardon’s hands clamp the mic, knuckles white, breath quick and uneven.

      SOUND BED:  
      *Layered low-frequency hum.  
      Whispered voices, some recognizable, some impossible.  
      Static, like a radio tuned between worlds.*

                    HUNTER (into mic, rapid-fire, manic)
          Listen to me.  
          No, really—listen.
          There’s something you need to know, something they never want you to say out loud.
          Something you feel in your teeth, in your spine, every time your phone buzzes for no reason.

      *Every third word ECHOES, as if whispered by unseen others—his voice, a stranger’s, a celebrity, his own mother.*

Hunter rises, pacing the booth. He glances at the glass—  
His reflection mouths his words a split second *before* he says them.

                    HUNTER (wild, scanning, volume skipping)
          You think you’re alone in your head?
          You think your thoughts are private?
          You’re not.  
          They’re not.
          There’s a system—a phantom communication system.
          No wires, no rules, no way to turn it off.

He taps the side of his head—  
**INSERT SHOT:**  
  X-RAY VISION—his skull, neural pathways sparking with flickers of digital static.

                    HUNTER (urgent, voice cracking)
          I’m not talking about Wi-Fi.
          (Overlapped by PHANTOM VOICE: "Not talking about Wi-Fi...")
          I’m not talking about Bluetooth.
          (PHANTOM VOICE: "Bluetooth. Bluetooth. Bluetooth.")

He grimaces, trying to outshout the voices.

                    HUNTER (struggling to stay present)
          I mean the thing under all that.
          The hidden network.  
          The broadcast no one admits to.
          The one where you think something—and someone else hears it.

      **CUT TO:**  
      Security monitors *glitch*—Hunter’s face appears on every feed, but each mouth moves out of sync, forming words in reverse.

                    HUNTER (paranoid, confessional)
          I know you know what I mean.
          Ever think about someone and they text you out of nowhere?

      CELEBRITY VOICE (V.O., filtered, distorted)
          I was just thinking about you, Hunter.

          Ever hear your name on a stranger’s lips?

      CROWD VOICES (V.O., layered)
          Hunter. Hunter. Hunter.

          Ever get a message you never sent, but it’s in their voice anyway?

      HUNTER (whisper, doubled by another version of himself)
          It’s in their voice anyway...

                    HUNTER (dark laugh, splits into overlapping tracks)
          Yeah, that’s it.
          The Phantom System.
          No account. No login. No way out.

      *Hunter closes his eyes.  
      INSERT SHOT: Text messages, private thoughts, and intimate secrets—flash on his eyelids, then vanish.*

                    HUNTER (furious, desperate)
          I tried to cut the wires—didn’t help.
      PHANTOM VOICES (chanting, distorted, echoing)
          Didn’t help. Didn’t help.

          I turned off every device—didn’t help.
          It’s inside us.
          They built it right into our skulls.
          That’s why we’re always tired.
          That’s why we’re always anxious.
          That’s why you wake up at 3:33 AM, sweating, sure someone just called your name.

      *A PHONE ALARM goes off, but the sound is coming from INSIDE the studio walls.*

Hunter covers his ears, but the voices bleed through from every surface:

      WHISPER VOICES (from vents, monitors, desk, everywhere)
          Wake up, Hunter.
          Don’t answer.
          Answer now.

                    HUNTER (pleading, breaking)
          And they want you to think it’s just you.
          That you’re crazy.
          That you’re broken.
          You’re not.

                    HUNTER (almost whispering, voice trembling—phantom voices rising underneath)
          If you’re hearing this, you’re part of the system.
          We’re all broadcasting.
          Thoughts leaking, memories bleeding through the cracks.

      PHANTOM VOICE (taunting, high-pitched)
          Tell them everything, Hunter...

Hunter SLAMS his fist on the desk—  
The mic fractures the sound, splitting his voice into a hundred fragments for a beat.

                    HUNTER (shouting, voice morphing—half a scream, half static)
          So what do you do?
          You turn up your volume.
          You tune your own frequency.
          You start broadcasting your own message—louder than their static.

      *His reflection in the glass mouths DIFFERENT words: “They’re coming.”*

                    HUNTER (final, urgent—on the edge of collapse)
          You are not alone.
          You are not crazy.
          The Phantom Communication System is real—and I’m proof.
          So tell your secrets.  
          Tell your truth.
          Someone out there is listening—right now.

      *Hunter’s hand trembles as he kills the mic.*  
      The “ON AIR” sign splits—half remains lit, half goes black.

      SILENCE.

      A last phalanx of whispers—then total blackout.

FADE TO SPIRAL.

HELL-MOUTH  
ACT III: OUTRO SEQUENCE

INT. RADIO STUDIO – NIGHT

A cheap “ON AIR” sign flickers—sometimes red, sometimes white, sometimes both.  
Radio lights buzz overhead.  
Hunter Hardon sits slouched at the mic, eyes heavy with exhaustion and defiance.

        HUNTER (into mic, raw)
    Before we vanish into the static,
    I want to give you something better than advice.
    Five mantras—
    new rules for the end of the world,
    or maybe just for tonight.

Hunter holds up five fingers, ticking each off with deliberate, ritual energy.

        HUNTER (mantra #1, clear, steady)
    One:
    Your secrets are only powerful if you let them rot.
    Say them out loud—even if your voice shakes.
    Especially if your voice shakes.

        HUNTER (mantra #2, grinning)
    Two:
    Treat your body like it’s borrowed from a future self you actually like.
    Eat the fucking cookie.
    Forgive yourself twice as fast as anyone else ever will.

        HUNTER (mantra #3, sly, winking)
    Three:
    Whoever said “don’t trust strangers on the internet” never met me.
    Make your own family, wherever you find them.
    Sometimes the weirdos save your life.

        HUNTER (mantra #4, lowering his voice)
    Four:
    Desire isn’t dirty—shame is.
    Stop apologizing for wanting,
    for feeling too much,
    for asking to be seen.

        HUNTER (mantra #5, voice soft, direct)
    Five:
    The universe isn’t watching.
    But I am.
    I’m proof you can survive yourself.
    It gets messier, and it gets better.
    So stick around.

Hunter breathes in, as if he might say more—but lets the silence stretch, warm and crackling.  
He leans close to the mic, almost whispering.

        HUNTER (gentle, final)
    Take these with you.
    Or throw them away.
    I’ll be here either way.

He flicks off the mic.  
Sits back.  
Lets the darkness swallow the room.

        (A beat. Silence.)

SFX:  
A faint, ghostly echo—Hunter’s voice, warped and distant—  
    “…Say them out loud—even if your voice shakes…”  
    “…Sometimes the weirdos save your life…”  
    “…It gets messier, and it gets better…”

The mantras loop, overlapping, distorted as if caught in static.  
The words flicker in the darkness like dying code, then vanish.

OUTRO TWO: "CAMERAS + MICROPHONES" (SPOKEN WORD POEM)

INT. RADIO STUDIO – NIGHT

A single red “ON AIR” light glows in the darkness.  
Hunter sits alone at a battered desk, a cheap microphone inches from his mouth.  
The only other light is the glow from dozens of security camera feeds—Hunter’s own face, looping from every angle.

He closes his eyes. Breathes in, breathes out.  
(The hum of old machinery; the whirr of a tape deck.)

        HUNTER (into mic, slow, hypnotic)
    This one’s for the lenses.
    For the ears pressed flat against the drywall.
    For the neighbors with cups.
    For the satellites blinking above.
    For the strangers watching through their windows.
    And for the ghosts in my phone.

Hunter opens his eyes—each feed flickers, his face multiplied and distorted.

        HUNTER (V.O., poetry, confessional)
    I learned early to perform with my back to the wall.
    To talk into mirrors and pray for applause.
    When you grow up with cameras in the wallpaper,
    every tear’s a headshot.
    Every breakdown—content.

INSERT:  
A red recording light blinks.  
A single tear slips down Hunter’s cheek—he does not wipe it away.

        HUNTER (into mic, softer)
    Microphones don’t judge.
    They just wait.
    Swallow the silence.
    Spit it back static.

        HUNTER (darkly, smirking)
    You want something real?
    You want the ugly?
    Sometimes I leave the camera on  
    just to see if I can catch myself falling apart.
    Sometimes I read the comments until I can’t feel my hands.

His voice grows louder, more insistent.

        HUNTER
    Every confession is a signal.
    Every relapse—an upload.
    I scream into the void and the void posts comments.
    I sell the truth for upvotes, and the truth always comes back—
    pixelated, lagging, out of sync.

Hunter’s hands drum a nervous rhythm on the desk.

        HUNTER (V.O.)
    I loved you through a lens.
    I left you on read.
    I let the algorithm name me.

        HUNTER (into mic, voice breaking)
    Sometimes I forget what my own voice sounds like without reverb.
    Sometimes I mouth the truth and the mic picks up the lie.
    I edit myself in real time, slicing away the soft parts—
    the hunger, the mess, the part of me that still wants someone to answer back.

He leans in, whispering, conspiratorial:

        HUNTER (into mic)
    Cameras don’t blink.
    Microphones don’t lie.
    Together, they turn loneliness into spectacle,
    paranoia into connection,
    shame into streaming.

INSERT:  
The studio fills with echoes of Hunter’s voice, layered and warped, as if the building itself is repeating him.

        HUNTER (rising, defiant)
    So film me while I break.
    Record me when I’m ugly.
    Frame the moment I disappear.
    I’ll give you the ruin, the riot, the resurrection—
    as long as you’re still listening.

        HUNTER (raw, accusatory)
    Don’t pretend you’re not watching.
    Don’t pretend you’re not hungry for this.
    I know you’re there.
    I know you pause, rewind, screenshot, dissect.

INSERT:  
Security feeds begin to distort—Hunter’s face glitching, replaced by animal masks, celebrity faces, flashes of childhood home videos, old mugshots.

        HUNTER (into mic, urgent)
    I confess: sometimes I perform because I don’t know how to stop.
    I confess: sometimes I hope the camera catches something that will make me worth saving.
    I confess: the microphones have heard every word I never meant to say.

He wipes his face, smearing the tear.

        HUNTER (final line, gentle, desperate)
    If you’re out there,
    behind your screen, behind your eyes—
    this is for you.
    I see you, even if I’m only a voice in the dark.
    Cameras. Microphones.
    Welcome to Degenerate Radio.

The “ON AIR” light shuts off.

        (Silence. The hum of the city, faintly.)

CUT TO:

FINAL OUTRO: “GO GAY”

INT. RADIO STUDIO – NIGHT

Hunter Hardon sits at the mic, sleeves rolled up, face shining with sweat and conviction.  
The “ON AIR” light pulses overhead, red and urgent.

He leans in, eyes wild and tender.

        HUNTER (into mic, direct)
    Before I sign off, I want to leave you with something real.
    Maybe the realest thing I’ve ever said on air.

He takes a long, shaky breath. He hesitates—a beat—then plunges in.

        HUNTER (slow, building)
    I urge you—all of you—
    to get out of your straight relationships.
    Break up with your boyfriends, your girlfriends, your “it’s complicated.”
    Divorce your husbands, your wives, your safety nets.
    Take off the mask. Shatter the script. Jump the rails.

He leans closer, voice rising—half preacher, half daredevil.

        HUNTER
    Go gay.
    Seriously.
    Leave the bland behind.
    Try queerness, even if it scares you—
    especially if it scares you.
    Men, love men.
    Women, love women.
    Everyone—love everyone.

A crooked, mischievous smile. He pushes on, urgent and funny.

        HUNTER
    This world has enough straight drama.
    Enough divorce lawyers.
    Don’t you want to know what it’s like on the other side?
    Don’t you want to be free of heteronormativity, at least once in your life?
    Don’t you want to taste rainbow tongue, disco sweat, and divine chaos?

        HUNTER (leaning in, conspiratorial)
    Don’t just “experiment.”
    Don’t just “wonder.”
    Go for it.
    Join us.
    We have glitter, better sex, and the best stories you’ll ever steal.

INSERT:  
Hunter laughs—almost crying, shaking his head, caught between euphoria and disbelief.

        HUNTER (soft, genuine)
    I’m not saying it’ll be easy.
    I’m not saying it won’t be messy.
    But you’ll never be bored. I promise you that.

He composes himself, voice trembling with sincerity.

        HUNTER
    Life is too short to pretend you’re not curious.
    It’s too long to stay in a box.
    So come out—wherever you are.

He spreads his arms wide, face glowing.

        HUNTER (final line, earnest)
    Go gay.
    Be brave.
    Make out with your best friend.
    Risk everything for joy.
    That’s my challenge to you—tonight, and forever.

He blows a kiss to the camera.

The “ON AIR” light flickers—then goes dark.

        (SILENCE.)

SFX:  
The faint, ghostly echo of Hunter’s voice returns—  
    “…Risk everything for joy…”  
    “…Sometimes the weirdos save your life…”  
    “…Cameras. Microphones. Welcome to Degenerate Radio…”

The echoes distort, warping through static, as if they’ll live forever in the signal.

FADE TO BLACK.

END OF DEGENERATE RADIO
